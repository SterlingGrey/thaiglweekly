# Selling the Ship

**In Thai GL the pair is the product. That is a business decision, and two people carry it.**

*From The Business of Love, a series of four essays. Opinion by Sterling Grey. Every factual claim is linked to its source. Corrections are published.*

---

Look at the name on a fan-meeting ticket. On one published listing, for an event in Bangkok on 30 March 2025, it is not the title of a series or the name of a character. It is *LOVE OVERLOAD: FREENBECKY 1st FANMEETING IN BANGKOK*. Two actresses, one word. ([ThaiTicketMajor listing](https://www.thaiticketmajor.com/concert/love-overload-freenbecky-1st-fanmeeting-in-bangkok-presented-by-dreamy-bubble-milk-tea.html))

That word is the business model of this genre.

I have worked in photography and writing since 1975. Selling with pictures is honest work. But a picture of a product and two young women sold as a product are different things, and the difference is what this essay is about.

Here is the model as anyone can see it from the outside. A studio casts two actresses together. If the chemistry works, it keeps them together: in the next series, on the press tour, at the fan meeting, in the brand campaign, on the photocard. The audience comes for a show and stays for a pair. The pair then earns in places the show never reaches.

The listing shows what the pair could be worth at the door. Its VIP tier was 20,000 baht (about $590). Seats were 5,500, 4,000 and 2,500 baht (about $165, $120 and $75). A live stream was 950 baht (about $28) and a rerun 750 (about $22). That is one event. It tells us nothing about what is typical, what the evening cost or who received the money. It does tell us what was on sale: the two of them, by name.

The model also shows its shape when it breaks. On 1 October, Motion Minds Entertainment announced that it was suspending Shelly Phetsai, ending her joint professional work with Aom Pundao and reviewing already scheduled work case by case. ([Motion Minds notice](https://x.com/MotionMindsEntt/status/2105628905092915400); [Thai GL Hub chronology](https://thaiglhub.com/the-making-and-unmaking-of-shellypundao/)) "What a Studio Owes Its Artists" deals with how that notice was written; here I read it only as a business document. A decision about one actress became a decision about a pairing, and about a calendar of commitments built on the two of them together. That is what it means to be sold as a unit. Half your working life is tied to somebody else's.

Now the part I cannot show you. Contracts in this industry are rarely published; here is what we can see. I have looked for evidence of what Thai GL contracts typically say about exclusivity, image rights, revenue shares or working hours, and I have found nothing that would let me tell you what is normal. So I will not tell you.

What surfaces instead are disputes. Thairath reported that on 10 July 2026 the Civil Court held mediation in Freen Sarocha Chankimha's contract claim against IDOLFACTORY, involving wages and revenue sharing of more than 8 million baht (about $237,000), and that it was not resolved that day. ([Thairath, 10 July 2026](https://www.thairath.co.th/entertain/news/2945432)) TNN reported on 5 March 2026 that Becky Armstrong said she had filed a lawsuit against her former agency over 20 million baht (about $590,000) in unpaid compensation. ([TNN, 5 March 2026](https://www.tnnthailand.com/entertainment/227544/)) These are claims. I have seen no ruling on either, and I make no finding here.

But look at what the claims are about. Not scripts, not scenes. Money each woman says she earned and was not paid, including, in one case, a share of revenue. When both names on that 2025 ticket turn up in the news over pay, the public gets a glimpse of the business underneath the romance. It is still more than the companies have chosen to show us.

I have found no published interviews in which the actresses describe their schedules, and I will not guess. What I can describe is what the model asks of anyone inside it.

It asks them to be one product with two faces. It asks each of them to tie her career to another person's choices, health and luck, and to another person's contract. It asks them to perform closeness off screen as well as on, at the fan meeting and in the campaign, because closeness is what the ticket sells. And it invites an audience to treat the time between the scenes as part of what it paid for. When that goes wrong, the actresses are the ones who have to live in it.

Now the fair line. Many fans enjoy the ship knowingly. They know the chemistry is craft, the same way they know a love song is a song. They buy the photocard because it is beautiful and because it is part of loving the thing. Nothing in this essay is aimed at them. The pleasure is real, and it is theirs.

My argument is with the companies. A business that sells two people as one owes those two people more than an ordinary contract, and it owes the public more than silence about what that contract says. Publish the principles, if not the terms: who owns a pair's image, what happens to one actress's work when the other's stops, how the money from a joint event is shared. I would like to name a studio that already does this, with a document to prove it. I have not found one. If you run that studio, send me the link and I will write about you.

This series asks three questions. Who pays, who profits, and what would a fairer model look like?
