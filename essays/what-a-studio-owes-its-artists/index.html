What a Studio Owes Its Artists

Motion Minds turned a private matter into a press release. That was the mistake, and it was theirs.

By Sterling Grey · 26 October 2026
Sources linked. Corrections published.

On 1 October, Motion Minds Entertainment published a notice about Shelly Phetsai. It suspended her from all activities, ended her joint professional work with Aom Pundao, and said existing commitments would be reviewed case by case. (the notice; a dated chronology)

I am not going to repeat the reason the notice gave. You may already know it. Half the internet thinks it does. It was personal, it belonged to two young women, and it was never ours to read. That is the whole point of this essay.

A company has every right to manage its business. When a schedule changes, partners need to know. Sponsors need to know. Fans who bought tickets need to know what happens to their money. That is professional information, and it fits in a short notice: what changes, from when, and who to contact.

Motion Minds went further. It put a personal reason into a public statement, with two names attached, on the permanent record. A private matter became a press release, and a press release is an invitation. The internet accepted it.

You saw what followed. Strangers picked sides. Two actresses who had given that studio their work, their faces and their names were dragged through comment sections in several languages. A company is supposed to stand between its artists and that kind of storm. This one opened the door and pointed at them.

That is my charge against Motion Minds, and it is a charge about conduct, not about anyone's heart. A studio's first duty, when something goes wrong in an artist's private life, is discretion. Say what changes. Keep the why private. Talk to your artists before you talk to the internet. Protect them first and your brand second, because without them there is no brand.

On 20 October, Motion Minds published a second notice: Shelly is suspended from work until 31 October and "will resume her regular duties starting November 1, 2026." (notice, 20 Oct 2026) To its credit, this one keeps the reasons private. But a return date is not an apology. It does not take back what the first notice put into the world, and it will reach a fraction of the people the first one did.

The new notice also asks everyone to follow the company's official channels "to prevent any misunderstandings arising from incomplete information." I agree with the principle. I would only point out where this misunderstanding began: on an official channel.

So what does a studio owe its artists?

It owes them discretion. A private life stays private, in every statement the company ever writes.

It owes them protection. When a pile-on starts, the company's job is to slow it down, not to feed it.

It owes them a contract that says so. No clause that lets an employer turn an adult's personal life into corporate business. Every artist signing with any studio in this industry should ask for that in writing, and every studio that wants good people should offer it first.

And Motion Minds, specifically, owes Shelly Phetsai and Aom Pundao an apology. Not a private one. A public one, as public as the notice that started this.

I have watched this industry for a long time, and I love what it makes. The shows are better than they have ever been. The way some studios treat the people who make them has not kept up. We are going to keep saying so, with names, with receipts, and without ever making the artists pay for it.

Where do you stand? Should a studio ever be allowed to explain an artist's private life in public? I say never. Tell me why I'm wrong: on X at @ThaiGLWeekly, or by email at hello@thaiglweekly.com.
